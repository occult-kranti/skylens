    Satellites are off.

      Online orbital-data checks are spaced at least two hours apart. Until the next check is due, this action uses the available cache. Checking does not turn the satellite layer on.

      The source, element age and next available check appear below each check.

      Enabling sends your selected latitude and longitude, rounded to 3 decimal places, to AvioADSB about every 12 seconds while this page is visible and the feed is enabled, including when you open another panel. Requests pause during simulated time and require a location you selected. No camera frames are sent. Community data is not for navigation.

      No account is required. AvioADSB allows anonymous access at one request per 10 seconds and 100 requests per day, shared with other users on your network. A full daily allowance lasts about 20 minutes at this app's 12-second interval. Polling stops when the API reports a limit; wait for the indicated time, then turn this feed off and on to retry.

        Alignment & camera calibration

        Sensor alignment is approximate. Match a known distant landmark or celestial object. Field of view is the estimated lens diagonal angle before the preview is cropped to fill the viewport. The projection accounts for that crop; camera zoom and rotation may require adjustment.

        Compass heading may be magnetic or uncertain. A manual correction is a calibration estimate, not a measured true-north solution. No visual recognition is performed.

        Methods, data & privacy

        SkyLens calculates positions from time, location, catalog data and sensors. A label does not establish that an object is visible through cloud or daylight. Camera frames remain on this device.

        Ephemerides & events: Astronomy Engine (MIT). Stars: HYG Database v4.1. Constellations & Messier objects: d3-celestial (BSD, © Olaf Frohn). Satellites: CelesTrak elements and satellite.js SGP4 (MIT). Aircraft: AvioADSB (CC BY 4.0).

        Source catalogs and orbital data may be incomplete or stale. Live camera alignment still needs calibration and physical-device verification.
